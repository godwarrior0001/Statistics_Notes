# Day 2: Measures of Central Tendency

## 1. Arithmetic Mean
* The sum of all observations divided by their count: x-bar = (sum of x_i) / n[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span). Highly sensitive to outliers.

## 2. Weighted Mean
* Applied when observations carry different importance weights (w_i): x_bar_w = sum(w_i * x_i) / sum(w_i)[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).

## 3. Median
* The middle value when data are sorted (Position = (n + 1) / 2)[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span). It is robust and unaffected by extreme outliers[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).

## 4. Mode
* The most frequently occurring value[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span). The only central measure usable for nominal data[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).

## AI / Data Science Connection
* Central tendency powers missing-value imputation: fill normal numeric features with the mean, skewed features (like income) with the median, and categorical features with the mode[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span).
